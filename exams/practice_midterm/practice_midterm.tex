\subsection*{1. Short Answer Questions (30 points)}
\vspace{5pt}
\question For each question, give a number, True/False/NEI, or a 1-2 sentence answer.
\vspace{5pt}
\begin{parts}
	\part[4] A monopolist faces demand with elasticity $\varepsilon = -4$ and has $MC = \$15$. What is the profit-maximizing price?

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] Consider the logit utility: $u_{ijt} = \beta \cdot \text{calories}_j + \alpha \cdot p_{jt} + \xi_{jt} + \varepsilon_{ijt}$. What is the economic interpretation of $\xi_{jt}$?

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] True, False, or NEI: In the logit model, if we remove one product from the market, the remaining products' market shares all increase proportionally.

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] Why are cost shifters (e.g., input prices) valid instruments for price? Explain both relevance and exogeneity. (1-2 sentences)

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] True, False, or NEI: In the logit model, cross-price elasticity between products $j$ and $k$ depends on how similar the products are.

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[3] True, False, or NEI: Ramsey pricing sets price equal to marginal cost for all goods.

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] A researcher claims that consumer surplus in logit is $CS = \delta_1 + \delta_2$. Consider a market with two goods and an outside option. Is this formula correct? Explain.

	\vspace{3cm}

\newpage

	\part[3] True, False, or NEI: In a self-selection problem with two types, the high-type's incentive compatibility constraint always binds at the optimum.

	\vspace{2cm}

\end{parts}

\subsection*{2. Demand Estimation (30 points)}
\question A researcher wants to estimate demand for smartphones using a logit model:
$$u_{ijt} = \beta \cdot \text{screen}_j + \alpha \cdot p_{jt} + \xi_{jt} + \varepsilon_{ijt}$$
where $\text{screen}_j$ is screen size (inches), $p_{jt}$ is price, $\xi_{jt}$ is unobserved product quality, and $\varepsilon_{ijt}$ is i.i.d. Type 1 Extreme Value.

\begin{parts}
	\part[5] Define the mean utility $\delta_{jt}$. Using the Berry inversion, derive an expression for $\delta_{jt}$ in terms of observed market shares $s_{jt}$ and the outside option share $s_{0t}$.

	\vspace{5cm}

	\part[5] Write down the linear regression equation the researcher would estimate to recover $\beta$ and $\alpha$. Clearly identify the dependent variable, regressors, and error term.

	\vspace{5cm}

	\part[5] Why does OLS produce biased estimates of $\alpha$? What is the likely direction of the bias? (2-3 sentences)

	\vspace{5cm}

\newpage

	\part[5] A researcher proposes using ``cost shifters'' (e.g., semiconductor chip prices) as instruments. Give one reason why these instruments might fail the exclusion restriction.

	\vspace{5cm}

	\part[5] Compared to the basic logit, what is one advantage of adding demographic interactions (e.g., age $\times$ screen size) to the model?

	\vspace{5cm}

	\part[5] A phone manufacturer launches two versions of the same phone: ``Phone Pro'' and ``Phone Pro Max,'' which are identical except for color. What will the logit model predict about consumer surplus from having both versions available? Is this prediction reasonable? Explain.

	\vspace{5cm}

\end{parts}

\subsection*{3. Two-Part Tariffs (20 points)}
\question A streaming service charges a monthly subscription fee $F$ and a per-movie price $p$. Marginal cost per movie is $c = 1$.

\textbf{Part A: Homogeneous consumers}

There are 200 identical consumers, each with demand $q = 30 - 3p$ for movies per month.

\begin{parts}
	\part[5] Write down the streaming service's profit as a function of $F$ and $p$. (Hint: What is consumer surplus at price $p$? What is the maximum $F$ the service can charge?)

	\vspace{6cm}

	\part[5] Show that the profit-maximizing per-movie price is $p^* = c = 1$. (Hint: Take the derivative of profit with respect to $p$ and set it equal to zero.)

	\vspace{6cm}

\end{parts}

\newpage

\textbf{Part B: Heterogeneous consumers}

Now suppose there are 100 ``movie buffs'' with demand $q_H = 20 - 2p$ and 100 ``casual viewers'' with demand $q_L = 8 - 2p$. Marginal cost is $c = 0$.

\begin{parts}
\setcounter{partno}{2}
	\part[10] The streaming service is considering two pricing strategies:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item[(a)] $F = 50$, $p = 0$
		\item[(b)] $F = 8$, $p = 0$
	\end{itemize}
	Calculate profit under each strategy. Which is better and why?

	\vspace{6cm}

\end{parts}

\subsection*{4. Self-Selection (20 points)}
\question An airline sells two ticket classes: Business (B) and Economy (E). There are two types of travelers: corporate travelers with high willingness to pay and leisure travelers with low willingness to pay.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Willingness to Pay} \\
Traveler Type & Business Class & Economy Class \\
\midrule
Corporate (50 travelers) & \$800 & \$300 \\
Leisure (100 travelers) & \$200 & \$150 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Marginal cost is \$50 for both classes.

\begin{parts}
	\part[4] If the airline could perfectly identify traveler types, what prices would it charge for each class to each type? What is total profit?

	\vspace{6cm}

	\part[4] The airline cannot identify types but offers both classes. Write down the incentive compatibility (IC) constraint that ensures corporate travelers buy Business rather than Economy.

	\vspace{4cm}

\newpage

	\part[6] Suppose the airline sets $P_B = \$750$ and $P_E = \$150$. Explain why these prices are not optimal. Which constraint is violated?

	\vspace{7cm}

	\part[6] Find the profit-maximizing prices for Business and Economy under self-selection. (Hint: Which IC constraint binds? Set it as an equality and solve.)

	\vspace{5cm}

\end{parts}

\end{questions}
