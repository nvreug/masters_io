\subsection*{1. Short Answer Questions (30 points)}
\vspace{5pt}
\question For each question, give a number, True/False/NEI, or a 1-2 sentence answer.
\vspace{5pt}
\begin{parts}
	\part[4] A monopolist faces demand with elasticity $\varepsilon = -2$ and has $MC = \$30$. What is the profit-maximizing price?

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] Consider the logit utility: $u_{ijt} = \beta \cdot \text{sugar}_j + \alpha \cdot p_{jt} + \xi_{jt} + \varepsilon_{ijt}$. What is the economic interpretation of $\alpha$?

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] True, False, or NEI: If we add 5 to the mean utility of all goods (including the outside option), the market shares do not change.

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] Why are BLP instruments (characteristics of other products) \textit{relevant} for price? (1-2 sentences)

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] True, False, or NEI: In the logit model with small market shares, higher-priced products typically have more elastic demand.

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[3] True, False, or NEI: Under average cost pricing ($P = AC$), deadweight loss is zero.

	\vspace{2cm}

	\part[4] A researcher claims that consumer surplus in logit is $CS = \frac{1}{|\alpha|} \left( \exp(\delta_1) + \exp(\delta_2) \right)$. Consider a market with two goods ($\delta_1 = \delta_2 = 2$) and an outside option. Is this formula correct? 

	\vspace{3cm}

\newpage

	\part[3] True, False, or NEI: Under perfect price discrimination, deadweight loss is zero.

	\vspace{2cm}

\end{parts}

\subsection*{2. Demand Estimation (30 points)}
\question A researcher wants to estimate demand for automobiles using a logit model:
$$u_{ijt} = \beta \cdot \text{mpg}_{j} + \alpha \cdot p_{jt} + \xi_{jt} + \varepsilon_{ijt}$$
where $\text{mpg}_j$ is fuel efficiency, $p_{jt}$ is price, $\xi_{jt}$ is unobserved product quality, and $\varepsilon_{ijt}$ is i.i.d. Type 1 Extreme Value.

\begin{parts}
	\part[6] Define the mean utility $\delta_{jt}$. Using the Berry inversion, derive an expression for $\delta_{jt}$ in terms of observed market shares $s_{jt}$ and the outside option share $s_{0t}$.

	\vspace{5cm}

	\part[6] Write down the linear regression equation the researcher would estimate to recover $\beta$ and $\alpha$. Clearly identify the dependent variable, regressors, and error term.

	\vspace{5cm}

	\part[6] A researcher proposes using ``Hausman instruments'' (prices of the same car in other markets) to address the endogeneity problem. Explain one reason why these instruments might be problematic.

	\vspace{5cm}

\newpage

	\part[6] Compared to the basic logit, what is one advantage of adding demographic interactions (e.g., income $\times$ price) to the model?

	\vspace{5cm}

	\part[6] A researcher takes all Toyota Corollas and splits them into two ``products'' based on the last digit of their license plate: ``Corolla-Odd'' and ``Corolla-Even.'' What will the logit model predict about the change in consumer surplus from this split? Should we believe this prediction? Explain.

	\vspace{5cm}

\end{parts}

\subsection*{3. Two-Part Tariffs (20 points)}
\question A gym charges a monthly membership fee $F$ and a per-visit price $p$. Marginal cost per visit is $c = 2$.

\textbf{Part A: Homogeneous consumers}

There are 100 identical consumers, each with demand $q = 20 - 2p$ for gym visits per month.

\begin{parts}
	\part[5] Write down the gym's profit as a function of $F$ and $p$. (Hint: What is consumer surplus at price $p$? What is the maximum $F$ the gym can charge?)

	\vspace{6cm}

	\part[5] Show that the profit-maximizing per-visit price is $p^* = c = 2$. (Hint: Take the derivative of profit with respect to $p$ and set it equal to zero.)

	\vspace{6cm}

\end{parts}

\newpage

\textbf{Part B: Heterogeneous consumers}

Now suppose there are 50 ``heavy users'' with demand $q_H = 10 - p$ and 100 ``light users'' with demand $q_L = 5 - p$. Marginal cost is $c = 0$.

\begin{parts}
\setcounter{partno}{2}
	\part[10] The gym is considering two pricing strategies:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item[(a)] $F = 50$, $p = 0$
		\item[(b)] $F = 12.5$, $p = 0$
	\end{itemize}
	Calculate profit under each strategy. Which is better and why?

	\vspace{6cm}

\end{parts}

\subsection*{4. Self-Selection (20 points)}
\question A software company sells two versions: Pro (P) and Basic (B). There are two types of users: businesses with high willingness to pay and students with low willingness to pay.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Willingness to Pay} \\
Consumer Type & Pro Version & Basic Version \\
\midrule
Business (40 users) & \$400 & \$150 \\
Student (60 users) & \$120 & \$100 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Marginal cost is \$30 for both versions.

\begin{parts}
	\part[6] If the company could perfectly identify consumer types, what prices would it charge for each version to each type? What is total profit?

	\vspace{6cm}

	\part[6] The company cannot identify types but offers both versions. Write down the incentive compatibility (IC) constraint that ensures businesses buy Pro rather than Basic.

	\vspace{4cm}

\newpage

	\part[8] Find the profit-maximizing prices for Pro and Basic under self-selection. (Hint: Which IC constraint binds? Set it as an equality and solve.)

	\vspace{5cm}

\end{parts}
