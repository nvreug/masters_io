\subsection*{2. Demand Estimation (30 points)}
\question Consider a market with 3 differentiated products. The logit demand model is:
$$u_{ijt} = \delta_{jt} + \alpha p_{jt} + \varepsilon_{ijt}$$
where $\alpha = -0.5$ is the price coefficient.

The following data are observed:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Product & Price ($p_j$) & Mean Utility ($\delta_{jt}$) \\
\midrule
1 & \$20 & 4.0 \\
2 & \$15 & 3.0 \\
3 & \$10 & 2.0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{parts}
	\part[8] Compute $v_j = \delta_{jt} + \alpha p_{jt}$ for each product. Then compute the market shares using:
	$$s_{jt} = \frac{\exp(v_j)}{1 + \sum_k \exp(v_k)}$$

	\vspace{5cm}

	\part[6] Compute the own-price elasticity for each product using $\eta_{jj} = \alpha p_j (1 - s_j)$. Which product has the most elastic demand?

	\vspace{4cm}

	\part[6] A researcher estimates demand using OLS (regressing $\ln(s_{jt}) - \ln(s_{0t})$ on price and product characteristics). Explain why this leads to biased estimates of $\alpha$. What is the direction of the bias?

	\vspace{4cm}

	\part[10] Using the log-sum formula, compute consumer surplus per consumer. If product 3 is removed from the market, what is the change in consumer surplus?
	$$CS = \frac{1}{|\alpha|} \ln\left(1 + \sum_j \exp(v_j)\right)$$

\end{parts}

\subsection*{3. Two-Part Tariffs (20 points)}
\question A gym charges a monthly membership fee $F$ and a per-visit price $p$. Marginal cost per visit is $c = 2$.

\textbf{Part A: Homogeneous consumers}

There are 100 identical consumers, each with demand $q = 20 - 2p$ for gym visits per month.

\begin{parts}
	\part[5] Write down the gym's profit as a function of $F$ and $p$. (Hint: What is consumer surplus at price $p$? What is the maximum $F$ the gym can charge?)

	\vspace{4cm}

	\part[5] Show that the profit-maximizing per-visit price is $p^* = c = 2$. (Hint: Take the derivative of profit with respect to $p$ and set it equal to zero.)

	\vspace{4cm}

\end{parts}

\textbf{Part B: Heterogeneous consumers}

Now suppose there are 50 ``heavy users'' with demand $q_H = 10 - p$ and 100 ``light users'' with demand $q_L = 5 - p$. Marginal cost is $c = 0$.

\begin{parts}
\setcounter{partno}{2}
	\part[10] The gym is considering two pricing strategies:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item[(a)] $F = 50$, $p = 0$
		\item[(b)] $F = 12.5$, $p = 0$
	\end{itemize}
	Calculate profit under each strategy. Which is better and why?

\end{parts}

\subsection*{4. Self-Selection (20 points)}
\question A software company sells two versions: Pro (P) and Basic (B). There are two types of users: businesses with high willingness to pay and students with low willingness to pay.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Willingness to Pay} \\
Consumer Type & Pro Version & Basic Version \\
\midrule
Business (40 users) & \$400 & \$150 \\
Student (60 users) & \$120 & \$100 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Marginal cost is \$30 for both versions.

\begin{parts}
	\part[4] If the company could perfectly identify consumer types, what prices would it charge for each version to each type? What is total profit?

	\vspace{3cm}

	\part[4] The company cannot identify types but offers both versions. Write down the incentive compatibility (IC) constraint that ensures businesses buy Pro rather than Basic.

	\vspace{3cm}

	\part[6] Suppose the company sets $P_P = \$380$ and $P_B = \$100$. Explain why these prices are not optimal. Which constraint is violated?

	\vspace{4cm}

	\part[6] Find the profit-maximizing prices for Pro and Basic under self-selection. (Hint: Which IC constraint binds? Set it as an equality and solve.)

\end{parts}
